Cognitive Changes Quick-Reference Guide

Compare the features of normal aging, dementia, and delirium using this quick-reference table.

Feature Normal Aging Dementia Delirium
Onset Gradual, part of aging Gradual (months–years) Sudden (hours–days)
Memory Minor forgetfulness, but recall improves with cues Significant memory loss, especially recent events; does not improve with cues Memory may be poor, but attention and focus most affected
Attention No major change Usually okay until later stages Poor, easily distracted
Orientation Fully oriented May become disoriented to time/place/person Often disoriented, fluctuates during the day
Thinking Speed Slower but logical Impaired reasoning, difficulty problem-solving Disorganized thinking, rambling speech
Language Occasional word-finding difficulty Word-finding problems, repeating stories/questions Speech may be incoherent or irrelevant
Alertness Stable Stable until late stages Fluctuates (alert at times, drowsy at others)
Mood/Behavior Stable personality Personality changes, irritability, withdrawal Anxiety, agitation, or lethargy
Cause Normal brain aging Neurodegenerative disease (Alzheimer’s, Lewy Body, etc.) Medical problem (infection, meds, dehydration, etc.)
Course Does not interfere with daily life Progressive decline, irreversible Temporary if cause treated
Reversibility Not needed No Often yes with treatment
